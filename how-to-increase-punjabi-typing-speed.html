How to increase Punjabi typing speed

Most candidates who fail the Punjabi typing test are not slow because their fingers are slow. They are slow because they look at the keyboard, stop to think about where a matra is, and go back to fix errors. Each of those habits can be fixed with the right kind of practice.

Accuracy first, then speed

In the Punjab test every wrong word costs 5 characters, so a fast but careless typist can end up with a lower net speed than a slow, careful one. Aim for 95% accuracy in practice before you try to go faster. Speed grows on its own once your fingers stop making the same mistakes, but accuracy rarely improves if you keep pushing for speed.

Learn to touch type, even if it feels slower at first

Touch typing means your fingers know where the keys are and your eyes stay on the passage. Candidates who look down at the keyboard lose their place in the passage several times a minute, and that alone can keep them below 25 words per minute.

  • Rest your left fingers on A S D F and your right fingers on J K L ;. The small bumps on F and J let you find this position without looking.
  • Each finger owns a column of keys. Press a key with its own finger and return to the home row.
  • Use the thumb for Space and the little finger of the other hand for Shift.

For a week or two you will type slower than before. That is normal. Push through it; within a month you will be faster than you ever were by looking.

Use the logic of the INSCRIPT layout

The INSCRIPT layout is not random. Vowel signs (matras) sit on the left hand and most consonants on the right, and the Shift key usually gives a related letter. Once you see the pattern, you remember keys instead of searching for them.

  • ਕ = K
  • ਖ = Shift+K (the aspirated pair is on Shift)
  • ਾ = E
  • ਆ = Shift+E (the full vowel is on Shift of its matra)

In Unicode Punjabi you type letters in the order you would say them, with one exception: sihari is typed after the consonant even though it appears before it. For example:

  • ਕਿਤਾਬ = K F L E Y

A daily routine that works

  1. Warm up for 5 minutes with the lesson you are on, or with your weakest keys. Akhar's weak keys drill builds this for you from your own mistakes.
  2. Practise words for 10 minutes. Common words such as ਅਤੇ, ਵਿੱਚ, ਨੂੰ, ਦੀ and ਹੈ make up a large share of any passage. When these are automatic, your speed jumps.
  3. Type one passage for 10 minutes in practice mode, which stops on every mistake so you build the right habit.
  4. Every second day, take a timed test and read the word-by-word result. Write down your three most common mistakes and drill them the next day.

Thirty minutes a day, every day, beats three hours once a week. Your fingers learn through repetition and sleep, not through long sessions.

Fix the mistakes that cost the most

  • Addak and tippi: forgetting ੱ in words like ਵਿੱਚ or ਪੱਕਾ is the most common full mistake.
  • Sihari order: typing sihari before the consonant gives a broken word.
  • Spacing: two words joined together count as a half mistake each time. Press Space once and only once.
  • Danda: type । straight after the last word, with no space before it.

The article on common Punjabi typing mistakes covers each of these in detail.

What speed to expect, and when

A candidate starting from zero who practises 30 to 45 minutes a day usually reaches 15 words per minute in about three weeks and 30 words per minute in two to three months. If you already type English by touch, it is often faster, because the finger movements carry over. Progress is not a straight line: expect weeks where nothing seems to improve, followed by a sudden jump.

Posture and setup

Speed problems are sometimes physical. If your wrists ache after ten minutes, you will slow down long before the test ends.

  • Sit straight with your feet flat on the floor and the screen at eye level, about an arm's length away.
  • Keep your elbows close to your body and your wrists level. Do not rest the heels of your hands on the desk while typing.
  • Strike keys lightly. Pressing hard tires your fingers and does not make you faster.
  • Practise on a full-size keyboard if you can. Laptop keyboards work, but exam centres usually have desktop keyboards, and the feel is different.

Read ahead, type in words

Beginners read one letter, type it, then look for the next. Faster typists read a word or two ahead and let their fingers type a whole word as one movement. You can train this on purpose: in practice mode, try to keep your eyes one word ahead of the word you are typing. It feels uncomfortable for a few days, then becomes natural, and it is one of the biggest single jumps in speed.

The same idea applies to common letter groups. Endings like ਿਆਂ, ੀਆਂ and ਦੇ appear again and again. When your fingers type these as one pattern, you stop thinking about each key.

Do not chase speed in every session

Split your practice into two kinds. In accuracy sessions, type slowly enough that you make almost no mistakes. In speed sessions, push a little faster than is comfortable for short bursts of one or two minutes, then return to your normal pace. Mixing both kinds through the week builds speed without building bad habits.

Common reasons speed stops improving

  • Looking at the keyboard. Even a quick glance breaks your rhythm. Cover your hands with a cloth for a few sessions if you cannot stop.
  • Fixing every error. In a test, going back three words to fix a matra can cost more time than the mistake costs marks.
  • Practising only lessons. Lessons teach keys; passages build speed. Once you know all the keys, most of your time should go to full passages.
  • Long gaps. Missing several days in a row undoes progress. Short daily practice is better.
