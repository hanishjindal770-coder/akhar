Punjab govt typing test: rules, scoring and tips

Many Punjab government posts, such as clerk, typist and data entry operator, have a typing test after the written exam. Here is how the Punjabi test is usually run and scored, based on published typing-test notices, and how to prepare for it.

Rules differ between recruitments and can change. Always read the official notice for your post. The figures below come from published notices and are used as Akhar's defaults.

The format

  • Font and keyboard: Punjabi is typed in the Unicode Raavi font on the INSCRIPT keyboard.
  • Duration: 10 minutes for Punjabi, usually followed by an English test of the same length after a short break.
  • Qualifying standard: at least 30 words per minute with at least 92% accuracy (no more than 8% mistakes).
  • The passage is shown on screen and you type it in a box below. There is no on-screen help.

How mistakes are counted

Full mistakes

  • Leaving out a word.
  • Typing a wrong word (substitution).
  • Adding a word that is not in the passage.
  • Leaving out punctuation.
  • A missing or extra matra, addak or half letter. In Punjabi this makes the whole word wrong.

Half mistakes

  • Spacing errors: two words joined together, or one word split in two.
  • Wrong spacing around punctuation.

The formula

error characters = mistakes × 5 net characters = gross characters − error characters gross speed (WPM) = gross characters ÷ 5 ÷ minutes net speed (WPM) = net characters ÷ 5 ÷ minutes accuracy = net characters ÷ gross characters × 100

Backspace, Shift and other function keys do not count as characters, and extra spaces are not counted either.

Worked example

In a 10-minute test you type 1,650 characters with 6 full mistakes and 2 half mistakes.

  • Mistakes = 6 + (2 × ½) = 7
  • Error characters = 7 × 5 = 35
  • Net characters = 1,650 − 35 = 1,615
  • Gross speed = 1,650 ÷ 5 ÷ 10 = 33 WPM
  • Net speed = 1,615 ÷ 5 ÷ 10 = 32.3 WPM
  • Accuracy = 1,615 ÷ 1,650 × 100 = 97.9%

This candidate qualifies: the speed is above 30 WPM and accuracy is above 92%. With 20 full mistakes instead, accuracy would fall to about 94% and still pass, but 35 full mistakes would push it below 92% and fail, even at the same speed. Accuracy matters as much as speed.

A 30-day plan

  • Days 1 to 7: Akhar lessons 1 to 23 (home, top and bottom rows). 30 minutes a day. Do not look at the keys.
  • Days 8 to 14: lessons 24 to 42 (Shift letters, addak, pair bindi, half letters, numerals). Repeat any lesson below 95% accuracy.
  • Days 15 to 21: word and sentence lessons, then practice passages. Take a 5-minute test every second day.
  • Days 22 to 30: a full 10-minute test every day with Backspace allowed, then once with Backspace turned off. Study the word-by-word result and drill your weak keys.

Tips for the exam day

  • Check that the keyboard is set to Punjabi before the timer starts. Type one letter to confirm.
  • Read a few words ahead, not one letter at a time.
  • Do not stop to fix every small error. A wrong word costs one mistake, but stopping costs speed. Fix errors only in the word you are typing.
  • Press Space once between words and never put a space before the danda ।.
  • Finish the word you are on when time is nearly up. A half-typed word counts as a mistake.
